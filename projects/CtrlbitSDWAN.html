Ctrlbit SD-WAN

An SD-WAN product for MikroTik hardware — dual-SIM cellular failover managed via GenieACS/TR-069, plus a network-boot path for running OpenWRT on RouterBOARD devices.

What is Ctrlbit SD-WAN?

Ctrlbit was a small SD-WAN venture I co-built with a family friend, targeting MikroTik hardware as a lower-cost alternative to commercial SD-WAN appliances. The pitch was straightforward: give small sites automatic failover between two cellular SIMs so a primary carrier outage doesn't take the site offline, and manage the whole fleet remotely instead of touching each router by hand.

Architecture

Fleet management and failover decisions went through GenieACS, a TR-069 auto-configuration server — TR-069 is the standard protocol ISPs use to remotely provision CPE hardware, and using it here meant we could push config changes and pull health telemetry across every deployed router from one place, rather than relying on per-device SSH access. The genieacs-dualsim (opens in new tab) repo captures the approach: a periodic connectivity check against the primary SIM which, on failure, pushes a TR-069 parameter change through GenieACS to switch the device onto its secondary SIM — failover was orchestrated from the ACS side rather than decided locally on the router.

A second, more exploratory piece of the work was a guide for network-booting OpenWRT onto RouterBOARD devices — MikroTik's hardware normally ships with RouterOS only. The OpenWRT-Initram-Flash (opens in new tab) repo documents the method used on a RouterBOARD 951Ui-2nD: a Vagrant VM on the same subnet as the router runs dnsmasq as a combined TFTP/BOOTP server, and serves the target OpenWRT initramfs image to the router over its RouterBOOT DHCP boot path — getting a different OS onto the hardware without a manual flash, for OpenWRT's broader package ecosystem versus RouterOS's more closed one.

Outcome

The product worked technically, but the venture wound down after import-tax changes made the underlying MikroTik hardware meaningfully more expensive to source — enough to break the cost story the whole pitch depended on. No shipped customers were left mid-deployment; the project was stopped at the hardware-sourcing stage.
